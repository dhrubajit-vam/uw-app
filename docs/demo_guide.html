The Underwriting Engine

A model-backed decisioning platform for US personal auto: three trained models, one deterministic rules engine, a suggestion layer that looks past a flat decline, and an AI narrator that explains the decision in plain English, without ever making it.

What This Is

One submission in, one full underwriting decision out

A new personal auto submission arrives, driver, vehicle, claims history, coverage detail. Today, deciding what to do with it means a human underwriter reads the file, applies judgment, and reaches a price and a verdict. That judgment is valuable, but it isn't fast and it isn't perfectly consistent between two different underwriters on two different days.

This platform runs the same submission through three trained models and a fully documented rules engine in under a second, and returns exactly what an underwriter would want to see: a risk band, a price, a recommended action, the reasoning behind all three, and, when the answer would otherwise be a flat decline, the alternatives a human underwriter would actually consider first.

It has two working tabs: a Dashboard for the existing 22,000-submission book, and an Interface for scoring one hypothetical submission live.

Why It's Trustworthy

The AI narrates. It never decides.

This is the single most important thing to say out loud in the room. Every number a client will see, the band, the premium, the score, the reason codes, comes from deterministic code that runs identically every time. The AI layer (Azure OpenAI, GPT-4o) is added on top, strictly to turn those already-computed facts into a readable paragraph. It is instructed never to introduce a number, a reason, or a recommendation that wasn't already computed, and if the AI service is ever unreachable, the app falls back to the plain rule-based text with nothing missing and nothing broken.

Deterministic core

Hard-stop rules, appetite scoring, premium build-up, and the composite-score weights are business policy written in code, the same inputs always produce the same output, and every one of those rules can be pointed to and read.

AI narrative layer

GPT-4o receives the finished figures and reason codes as ground truth and is instructed to only phrase and prioritize them. It writes the summary paragraph; it does not touch the math.

How It Decides

Three models feed one governed pipeline

Two of the three model outputs are risk (frequency, severity); one is commercial (bind likelihood). They never see each other's inputs, a documented no-leakage rule, and none of them ever sees a post-outcome field. The rules engine is the only place decisions get made.

The composite score

Composite = 50% × (100 − Loss Propensity) + 20% × Bind Propensity + 30% × Appetite , loss risk dominates by design; a high bind likelihood alone can never push a bad risk into an approval. Bands: 72+ Preferred · 55–71 Standard · 38–54 Non-Standard · below 38 Decline, unless a hard-stop rule fires first, in which case the score is forced to reflect it regardless of the raw math.

Suggestions

Beyond decline

A "Decline" band or a triggered hard-stop rule doesn't automatically end the conversation. The engine checks a second question first: is this reason genuinely non-negotiable, or is there a real underwriting move here? Only three reasons are treated as truly mandatory , everything else gets a re-scored alternative.

Modify Terms

Raise deductibles, or correct a misclassified business-use vehicle, re-rated through the same engine, not a guess.

Price-Up / Non-Standard

Quote at a non-standard load, or route to a specialty/E&S placement partner instead of a flat no.

Conditional Acceptance

Require telematics enrollment, a monitored lapse period, or a vehicle inspection, then re-evaluate.

What's actually non-negotiable

DUI/DWI conviction, license suspension, and a confirmed fraud indicator. Every other hard-stop reason, coverage lapse, undeclared business use, a vehicle value over the carrier's cap, prior total loss, a state outside the appetite footprint, gets a specific alternative, not a form letter.

Suggestions, Continued

The same risk, priced under every coverage option

Every scored submission also gets a Coverage Fit table: the identical risk, re-run through the full pipeline once per coverage option (UM/UIM Only, Liability Only, Comprehensive Only, Collision Only, Full Coverage). Coverage isn't treated as a footnote adjustment here, it changes the actual Collision/Comprehensive/UM-UIM flags the loss models read, so each row carries its own technical premium, quoted premium, appetite, and composite score, not a shared number with a label changed.

When a narrower coverage form gives the same risk a better band or a meaningfully better score, the table surfaces it as a named suggestion with the real premium delta attached, e.g. "Consider writing this as Comprehensive Only: composite 62.5 becomes 71.2, quoted premium $185 lower." When nothing beats the current selection, or a hard-stop rule applies regardless of coverage, it says so plainly instead of forcing a recommendation that doesn't hold up.

One honest edge case worth knowing before someone else finds it live: on a renewal, pricing is held within a guardrail band of last year's premium (60%-190%). If every coverage option's underlying cost still falls outside that band, all five quote to the identical capped number, that's the guardrail binding uniformly, not a bug, and the app says so directly under the table when it happens.

Explainability

Real reason codes, not a guess at them

The loss and bind reason codes are not an approximation layered on afterward, they are XGBoost's and LightGBM's own native contribution output, the exact same mechanism the third-party shap library wraps for tree models. The appetite reason codes are an exact read of the rules engine's own penalty formula. Nothing here is estimated.

Each panel is one horizontal bar chart: bars right of center make the outcome worse, bars left make it better, longest driver at the top. Deliberately plain, no relativities or log-space math on screen, just what moved the number and by how much.

Two safeguards worth knowing

  • If a field was never actually observed for this submission (e.g. a telematics score for a driver who isn't enrolled), it's excluded from the named reason codes rather than shown as if it were real. It's still accounted for in "all other factors" behind the scenes, so the underlying decomposition (visible in Technical & audit detail) still adds up exactly to the real prediction.
  • The age input accepts any realistic value, but the training book's oldest driver is 85. Past that point the app tells you the model is extrapolating, not applying a calibrated pattern, rather than presenting an unproven number with the same confidence as a real one.

Anticipated Questions

Likely questions in the room

Is the AI actually making the underwriting decision?

No. The band, premium, and every reason code are computed before the AI is ever called. The AI writes a paragraph explaining those already-final numbers, it has no path to change them.

What happens if the AI service is down during the meeting?

Nothing breaks. Every AI call is wrapped so a failure of any kind returns silently, and the app falls back to the plain rule-based summary and recommendation cards it already had. The scoring and recommendation math never depends on the AI being reachable.

Is this auditable for a regulator?

Every hard-stop reason, premium load, and score weight is a named constant in the rules engine, not a learned parameter. The loss and bind reason codes are exact model contributions, not a post-hoc estimate. The "Technical & audit detail" panel on every scored submission exposes the full underlying figures.

What data is this trained and demonstrated on?

A 22,000-row synthetic personal-auto book, no real persons, vehicles, or policies. It exists to demonstrate the mechanism end to end; production deployment would retrain against a carrier's real book under the same no-leakage governance rules already enforced here.
